### Q1: When and why does SIRCL succeed or fail? (Marginal gains and occasional degradation in Table 1)

Thank you for this important question. We have conducted extensive sample-level analyses to precisely characterize SIRCL's success and failure conditions.

Within our Table 1, where all methods share the same backbone, data pipeline, optimization schedule, and LoRA config, SIRCL consistently improves performance. On the CODI backbone, CODI+SIRCL outperforms both the CODI baseline and CODI+SIM-CoT on all three benchmarks (GSM8K: 55.3% vs 53.2%; SVAMP: 60.4% vs 60.2%; GSM-Hard: 13.3% vs 12.4%). Combining both techniques (CODI+SIM-CoT+SIRCL) achieves the highest overall performance (56.1% on GSM8K), confirming that SIRCL and SIM-CoT are complementary. The gain becomes even more pronounced under scaling — without SIRCL, SIM-CoT degrades from 53.22% to 43.06% as $T$ grows from 6 to 32; with SIRCL, accuracy instead *improves* to 57.01%, widening the advantage to nearly 14pp.

**When SIRCL fails.** We identify two failure modes:

1. **When does SIRCL fail?** The failure boundary suggested by our data is the case where the latent chain contracts too early: later states have very low radius, diversity, and path length, so the centroid is no longer summarizing an active reasoning process, but only the center of an already impoverished trajectory. In that regime, geometric regularization can stabilize the path, but it cannot recover reasoning information that has already been lost. Sample 175 illustrates this clearly: its trajectory shrinks from early radius 11.31 to late radius 3.16, with mean radius 4.12, diversity 6.18, and path length 48.80, and both baseline and +SIRCL remain wrong (10 and 0.5 vs. gold 15). Sample 935 shows the same pattern even more sharply (11.24 -> 2.18, mean radius 4.02, diversity 6.21, path length 54.31).

At the same time, we do not claim that every unrecoverable sample is of this form. Some still-wrong trajectories remain geometrically large, e.g., sample 268 (radius 14.50, diversity 20.86, path length 324.78). Thus unrecoverable failures are heterogeneous. Our narrower claim is that **SIRCL becomes weak whenever the centroid no longer carries useful task information**, and early contraction is the most directly supported and most relevant boundary for explaining the Coconut results.

**Case contrast.** Sample 220 illustrates the recoverable regime: baseline predicts 80 instead of the gold 70, with radius 14.75 and path length 326.78; after SIRCL, the answer becomes 70 while the trajectory contracts to radius 5.64 and path length 81.93. In other words, SIRCL helps when a rich trajectory drifts but still preserves enough structure to be pulled back. It fails when the trajectory has already become too compressed to contain useful corrective signal.

**Connecting to Coconut.** . In the Coconut training configuration we actually use, reasoning is introduced by a stage-wise replacement curriculum: for a 3-step rationale, training moves from 3 explicit segments, to 1 latent stage + 2 explicit segments, then 2 + 1, and finally 3 latent stages + 0 explicit segments. Since each latent stage uses 2 latent tokens, the final fully implicit chain contains only 6 latent tokens. This means Coconut constructs reasoning through a short segment-level latent chain rather than a longer end-to-end latent trajectory. As a result, the centroid is more likely to be computed from a trajectory that is already too short or too compressed to retain strong task-relevant information. Therefore, the smaller gains on Coconut are better interpreted as arising from **a higher proportion of trajectories whose centroids are no longer sufficiently informative**, rather than from any intrinsic incompatibility between SIRCL and Coconut.

**When SIRCL succeeds.** SIRCL's effectiveness depends on whether the baseline's latent trajectory still retains sufficient geometric structure (i.e., adequate spatial spread and token diversity). Our sample-level transition analysis on SIM-CoT (T=6, GSM8K, N=1319) reveals:

| Transition | Count | Proportion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Both correct | 627 | 47.5% |
| Recovered (wrong→correct) | 119 | 9.0% |
| Both wrong | 493 | 37.4% |
| Regressed (correct→wrong) | 80 | 6.1% |

The net effect is positive: 119 recovered vs. 80 regressed. Among baseline-wrong samples, SIRCL rescues **19.4%** from wrong to correct. Critically, the recovered samples have *intermediate* geometric properties (radius_mean=11.77, diversity=18.73) — they retain enough structure for SIRCL's centripetal constraint to redirect, but were drifting without guidance.

### Q2: Scaling beyond T=32 — where are the limits?

This is an excellent question that directly addresses our central claim.
由于我们在实验中看到 16个T的时候已经出现了性能最高，32个t的时候 达到了性能下降，所以没有继续。为了更清楚的认识到sircl在不同的T上的贡献，

 We have now conducted matched scaling experiments with and without SIRCL on the same backbone (SIM-CoT, LLaMA-1B-Instruct, GSM8K), with identical training data and optimizer settings:

**Table R1: Matched scaling comparison (GSM8K accuracy)**

| T | w/o SIRCL | + SIRCL | Δ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | 53.22% | 56.10% | +2.88 pp |
| 16 | 44.50% | 58.00% | **+13.50 pp** |
| 32 | 43.06% | 57.01% | **+13.95 pp** |

**Table R2: Degradation from T=6 baseline**

| Condition | T=6→T=16 | T=6→T=32 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| w/o SIRCL | −8.72 pp (degrading) | −10.16 pp (degrading) |
| + SIRCL | +1.90 pp (stable) | +0.91 pp (stable) |

Key observations:

1. **Without SIRCL, performance degrades monotonically** with T (53.22%→44.50%→43.06%, cumulative −10.16 pp), confirming that unconstrained implicit reasoning suffers from accumulated drift.
2. **With SIRCL, performance remains stable or slightly improves** (56.10%→58.00%→57.01%), demonstrating effective drift prevention.
3. **SIRCL's gain amplifies with T**: from +2.88 pp at T=6 to +13.50 pp at T=16 and +13.95 pp at T=32. This is consistent with the geometric intuition — longer chains accumulate more drift, creating more "correction space" for SIRCL.
4. **The marginal gain from T=16 to T=32 is small** (+13.50→+13.95 pp), suggesting that the performance is beginning to plateau around T=16–32 even with SIRCL. This indicates that while SIRCL prevents degradation, additional latent steps beyond a certain point provide diminishing reasoning capacity gains for the current model scale (1B parameters).

Regarding whether we have explored even larger T: our current experiments reach T=32, which is already the largest latent token budget reported in the implicit reasoning literature to our knowledge. The plateau behavior from T=16 to T=32 suggests that for 1B-scale models on GSM8K-level tasks, T≈16–32 approaches the effective reasoning capacity ceiling. We hypothesize that larger models with richer latent representations would benefit from even longer chains, and we note this as a promising direction for future work.

Importantly, the fact that **the marginal gains in Table 1 (T=6) are small is itself consistent with our theory**: at T=6, there is limited drift to correct, so SIRCL's benefit is modest (+2.88 pp). The true value of SIRCL emerges precisely when scaling to larger T, where the alternative (no regularization) leads to severe performance collapse. This reframes the "marginal gain" not as a weakness but as evidence that SIRCL's primary contribution is *enabling stable scaling* rather than boosting short-chain performance.
