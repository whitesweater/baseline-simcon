## Q1: Lack of Theoretical Explanation; Concern That Centripetal Constraint May Suppress Diversity and Cause Model Collapse

We appreciate this important question. We agree that SIRCL is best characterized as a principled heuristic grounded in geometric intuition, rather than a method derived from first principles of Transformer theory. We position it accordingly.

The key geometric phenomenon we observe without SIRCL is a **zig-zag trajectory**: latent states do not move steadily toward the final reasoning state, but repeatedly overshoot, turn back, and wander before converging. This interpretation is also consistent with the recent analysis of Anderson (2026), who reports that across domains, scales, and tasks, step-to-step coherence remains stably negative, indicating that reasoning trajectories naturally zig-zag through latent space rather than flow smoothly toward a fixed attractor. Anderson further argues that this is likely an **architectural signature of Transformer residual-stream dynamics**, rather than a task-specific artifact: attention pulls representations toward contextually relevant states, while feedforward layers push them toward task-appropriate transformations, and the interplay of these two effects naturally produces oscillation. This is exactly the regime we aim to regularize. The goal of SIRCL is therefore not to eliminate all movement or force a straight-line trajectory, but to suppress **unproductive oscillatory wandering** while preserving useful local exploration.

Appendix D gives direct evidence for this interpretation. To isolate the effect of SIRCL on **trajectory quality** rather than end-task accuracy, we analyze the **526 GSM8K samples correctly solved by all four variants**. Based on these matched samples, we draw three conclusions.

**1. SIRCL suppresses oscillatory wandering and produces more direct trajectories.** Without SIRCL, CODI shows strong non-monotonicity in its distance-to-final-token curves (Figure 8), and in Figure 7 it accumulates about **107** units of total movement for only about **5** units of net displacement. After adding SIRCL, the total path length drops by **66.7%** to about **36**, meaning much more latent movement contributes to actual progress instead of detours.

**2. SIRCL yields earlier and more monotonic convergence to the final reasoning state.** The distance-to-final-token curves become smaller and tighter across iterations, and the convergence-rate improvement reaches **+457.4%** for CODI and **+15.9%** for SIM-CoT. This indicates that the trajectory approaches the solution both faster and more steadily.

**3. SIRCL produces globally more coherent intermediate states.** In the inter-iteration cosine heatmaps (Figure 10), CODI without SIRCL looks fragmented, whereas CODI+SIRCL forms a much more coherent high-similarity block across iterations 2-7. Combined with the large smoothness gains, this shows that SIRCL reduces directional drift and yields more self-consistent latent reasoning.

These conclusions are summarized quantitatively below:

| Metric | CODI → +SIRCL | SIM-CoT → +SIRCL |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cosine sim. to final state ↑ | +19.9% | +9.6% |
| Distance to final state ↓ | −58.9% | −43.5% |
| Cluster compactness ↓ | −52.1% | −37.9% |
| Trajectory smoothness ↑ | +58.6% | +245.0% |
| Total path length ↓ | −66.7% | −41.7% |
| Convergence rate (slope mag.) ↑ | +457.4% | +15.9% |

Taken together, Appendix D shows that SIRCL does not merely improve accuracy; it makes latent-space reasoning **shorter, more direct, faster-converging, and more self-consistent**.

This also clarifies why we do **not** view SIRCL as causing model collapse. The key design choice is the **hinge-style trust region** (Eq. 5): SIRCL only penalizes latent tokens that drift *beyond* radius $R$ from the centroid, while leaving tokens inside the feasible region **completely unconstrained**. This is fundamentally different from a continuous L2 pull (such as center loss), which would always compress representations toward a point.

We offer two arguments — one structural, one empirical — for why SIRCL does not lead to collapse:

**Structural argument: the hinge gradient is exactly zero inside the trust region.** For any token $z_t$ with $d(z_t, \mu) \leq R$, the SIRCL loss contributes zero gradient. This means SIRCL *cannot* compress tokens that are already within the feasible region — it has no mechanism to pull them closer to the centroid. The only tokens that receive a corrective gradient are those that have drifted *unusually far*. In contrast, true collapse would require a persistent inward force on all tokens (as in standard L2 regularization or center loss), which the hinge design explicitly excludes. Furthermore, the centroid $\mu$ is computed **per-sample on the fly** from the current trajectory — there is no shared global attractor across the dataset. Different samples are free to have completely different trajectory geometries; SIRCL only constrains each trajectory relative to its own center.

**Empirical argument: if SIRCL caused collapse, longer chains would perform worse, not better.** The most direct test of the collapse hypothesis is scaling. If SIRCL were progressively destroying representational diversity, then applying it over more latent iterations ($T$) should amplify the damage — we would expect accuracy to degrade as $T$ grows. The opposite is observed:

| $T$ | SIM-CoT (no SIRCL) | SIM-CoT+SIRCL | $\Delta$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | 53.22% | 56.10% | +2.88pp |
| 16 | 44.50% | **58.00%** | **+13.50pp** |
| 32 | 43.06% | **57.01%** | **+13.95pp** |

Without SIRCL, longer chains degrade by nearly 10pp ($T$=6→32), consistent with accumulated drift. With SIRCL, accuracy *improves* as $T$ grows and the advantage widens to nearly 14pp. This scaling behavior is **incompatible with collapse**: a collapsing regularizer would amplify degradation at larger $T$, not reverse it. Instead, SIRCL's hinge constraint acts as a stabilizer that bounds drift accumulation while leaving the model's internal exploratory dynamics intact.

The geometric intuition is therefore straightforward: SIRCL does not try to flatten the trajectory into a straight line. Instead, it suppresses **unproductive zig-zag drift** and keeps the latent chain inside a sample-specific trust region. This is why the observed effect is not collapse, but **more efficient and more coherent latent reasoning**.

## Q3: Performance Gap Between SIRCL and SIM-CoT [1]

We thank the reviewer for the careful cross-paper comparison. We address this from three aspects.
We reproduced SIM-CoT following the methodology described in [1] and using the authors' official open-source codebase. The small numerical differences between our reproduced results and those reported in [1] may be due to differences in hardware and software environment, including the GPU platform and corresponding CUDA/PyTorch versions. In our experiments, all methods in Table 1 are trained under the same conditions, which ensures a consistent comparison within our table.

### 1. SIRCL does provide measurable gains under matched conditions

Within our Table 1, all methods share the same backbone, data pipeline, optimization schedule, and LoRA setting. Under this matched setup, the gains from SIRCL are clearer when written directly as a table:

| Method | GSM8K | CommonsenseQA | SVAMP | GSM-Hard | ASDiv |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CODI | 52.9 | 70.3 | 59.9 | 12.1 | 65.4 |
| CODI+SIM-CoT ("SIM-CoT" in Table 1) | 53.2 | 71.0 | 60.2 | 12.4 | 64.5 |
| CODI+SIRCL | 55.3 | 70.8 | 60.4 | 13.3 | 64.1 |
| CODI+SIM-CoT+SIRCL ("SIM-CoT+SIRCL" in Table 1) | **56.1** | **71.8** | **60.6** | 12.7 | **65.5** |

This table suggests two useful observations. First, compared with **CODI+SIM-CoT**, **CODI+SIRCL** improves performance on **GSM8K** (**55.3 vs. 53.2**), **SVAMP** (**60.4 vs. 60.2**), and **GSM-Hard** (**13.3 vs. 12.4**), while showing only small decreases on **CommonsenseQA** (**70.8 vs. 71.0**) and **ASDiv** (**64.1 vs. 64.5**).. Second, when SIRCL is added on top of SIM-CoT, the combined model improves all five columns. Overall, these results suggest that SIRCL provides a useful training-time regularization effect and can work together with SIM-CoT, while introducing no additional inference-time cost and only a small training overhead in time and memory.

The gain also becomes much more pronounced under scaling:

| $T$ | SIM-CoT (no SIRCL) | SIM-CoT+SIRCL | $\Delta$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | 53.22% | 56.10% | +2.88pp |
| 16 | 44.50% | **58.00%** | **+13.50pp** |
| 32 | 43.06% | **57.01%** | **+13.95pp** |

Without SIRCL, SIM-CoT degrades substantially as $T$ grows. With SIRCL, performance instead remains stable and much stronger. This is the main matched-setting evidence that SIRCL provides a real benefit rather than a presentation artifact.

### 2. Why SIRCL's gains on Coconut are smaller: a failure mode analysis

We honestly acknowledge that SIRCL's gains on Coconut are smaller than on CODI. Our revised interpretation is that the key question is **when the trajectory centroid remains informative enough for SIRCL to help**.

**When does SIRCL fail?** The failure boundary suggested by our data is the case where the latent chain contracts too early: later states have very low radius, diversity, and path length, so the centroid is no longer summarizing an active reasoning process, but only the center of an already impoverished trajectory. In that regime, geometric regularization can stabilize the path, but it cannot recover reasoning information that has already been lost. Sample 175 illustrates this clearly: its trajectory shrinks from early radius 11.31 to late radius 3.16, with mean radius 4.12, diversity 6.18, and path length 48.80, and both baseline and +SIRCL remain wrong (10 and 0.5 vs. gold 15). Sample 935 shows the same pattern even more sharply (11.24 -> 2.18, mean radius 4.02, diversity 6.21, path length 54.31).

At the same time, we do not claim that every unrecoverable sample is of this form. Some still-wrong trajectories remain geometrically large, e.g., sample 268 (radius 14.50, diversity 20.86, path length 324.78). Thus unrecoverable failures are heterogeneous. Our narrower claim is that **SIRCL becomes weak whenever the centroid no longer carries useful task information**, and early contraction is the most directly supported and most relevant boundary for explaining the Coconut results.

**Case contrast.** Sample 220 illustrates the recoverable regime: baseline predicts 80 instead of the gold 70, with radius 14.75 and path length 326.78; after SIRCL, the answer becomes 70 while the trajectory contracts to radius 5.64 and path length 81.93. In other words, SIRCL helps when a rich trajectory drifts but still preserves enough structure to be pulled back. It fails when the trajectory has already become too compressed to contain useful corrective signal.

**Connecting to Coconut.** In the Coconut training configuration we use, reasoning is constructed **stage by stage** rather than as one long end-to-end latent chain: training moves from fully explicit segments to partially latent stages, and the final fully implicit chain has only **6 latent tokens** in total. Because the latent trajectory is built in segments, its **global trajectory coherence is weaker** than in CODI or SIM-CoT. As a result, the centroid is a less reliable summary of the full reasoning process. We therefore interpret the smaller gains on Coconut as arising from **weaker global trajectory consistency and a less representative centroid**.
