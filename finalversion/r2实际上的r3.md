## Response to Reviewer

We sincerely thank the reviewer for the thoughtful and constructive evaluation. We are encouraged that you recognize the importance and practicality of SIRCL. Below we address each concern in detail.

---

### Q1: When and why does SIRCL succeed or fail? (Marginal gains and occasional degradation in Table 1)

Thank you for this important question. We agree that the gains in Table 1 are not uniformly large, and that a few dataset/baseline combinations show small degradations. Two broader patterns are nevertheless clear. First, SIRCL is **additive** to existing implicit-reasoning methods rather than replacing them: on the CODI backbone, CODI+SIRCL improves over CODI+SIM-CoT on **GSM8K** (**55.3 vs. 53.2**), **SVAMP** (**60.4 vs. 60.2**), and **GSM-Hard** (**13.3 vs. 12.4**), and combining both techniques yields the strongest GSM8K result (**56.1**). Second, the gain becomes much more pronounced when the reasoning chain is made longer: without SIRCL, SIM-CoT degrades from **53.22%** to **43.06%** as $T$ grows from 6 to 32, whereas with SIRCL it remains strong at **57.01%**, widening the advantage to nearly **14 points**.

When SIRCL succeeds, the effect is straightforward: it makes the latent reasoning process more efficient and more stable, so the model converges faster and with less unproductive wandering. In our geometric analysis, this appears as shorter paths, smoother trajectories, and earlier convergence.

The main question, then, is why SIRCL sometimes fails. The clearest failure mode suggested by our data is **early contraction**. In these cases, the latent chain contracts too early, so later states have very low radius, diversity, and path length. The centroid then no longer summarizes an active reasoning process, but only the center of an already impoverished trajectory. In that regime, geometric regularization can stabilize the path, but it cannot recover reasoning information that has already been lost. Sample **175** illustrates this clearly: its trajectory shrinks from early radius **11.31** to late radius **3.16**, with mean radius **4.12**, diversity **6.18**, and path length **48.80**, and both baseline and +SIRCL remain wrong (**10** and **0.5** vs. gold **15**). Sample **935** shows the same pattern even more sharply (**11.24 -> 2.18**, mean radius **4.02**, diversity **6.21**, path length **54.31**).

By contrast, sample **220** illustrates the recoverable regime: baseline predicts **80** instead of the gold **70**, with radius **14.75** and path length **326.78**; after SIRCL, the answer becomes **70** while the trajectory contracts to radius **5.64** and path length **81.93**. This is the type of case where SIRCL helps most: the trajectory is wrong, but it still retains enough usable structure to be redirected.

We also do not claim that every unrecoverable sample is an early-contraction case. Some still-wrong trajectories remain geometrically large, e.g., sample **268** (radius **14.50**, diversity **20.86**, path length **324.78**). So unrecoverable failures are heterogeneous. Our narrower claim is that **SIRCL becomes weak whenever the centroid no longer carries useful task information**, and early contraction is the most directly supported boundary in our current analysis.

This also helps explain why gains are smaller on Coconut. In the Coconut training configuration we use, reasoning is constructed stage by stage rather than as one long end-to-end latent chain, and the final fully implicit chain contains only **6 latent tokens**. Because the latent trajectory is built in segments, its global trajectory coherence is weaker, and the centroid is a less reliable summary of the full reasoning process. We therefore interpret the smaller gains on Coconut as arising from **weaker global trajectory consistency and a less representative centroid**, rather than from any intrinsic incompatibility between SIRCL and Coconut.

### Q2: Scaling beyond T=32 — where are the limits?

This is an excellent question, and it directly targets what we believe is the main contribution of SIRCL.

In our experiments, performance already peaks at **T = 16**, and shows a small decline at **T = 32**, so we did not continue to even larger `T` in this rebuttal. Instead, we prioritized the more important missing comparison: a **matched scaling study with and without SIRCL** under the same backbone, training data, and optimizer setting.

**Table R1: Matched scaling comparison (GSM8K accuracy)**

| T | w/o SIRCL | +SIRCL | Delta |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 6 | 53.22% | 56.10% | +2.88 pp |
| 16 | 44.50% | 58.00% | **+13.50 pp** |
| 32 | 43.06% | 57.01% | **+13.95 pp** |

**Table R2: Change relative to T=6**

| Condition | T=6 -> T=16 | T=6 -> T=32 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| w/o SIRCL | -8.72 pp | -10.16 pp |
| +SIRCL | +1.90 pp | +0.91 pp |

These matched results suggest four points.

1. **Without SIRCL, performance degrades monotonically as T grows** (`53.22 -> 44.50 -> 43.06`), which is consistent with accumulated drift in long latent chains.
2. **With SIRCL, performance remains stable and much stronger** (`56.10 -> 58.00 -> 57.01`), showing that SIRCL effectively prevents this degradation.
3. **SIRCL's gain amplifies with T**: from `+2.88 pp` at `T=6` to more than `+13 pp` at `T=16` and `T=32`. This is exactly what we would expect if longer chains create more drift and therefore more room for correction.
4. **The gain from T=16 to T=32 is small**, and the absolute performance slightly declines at `T=32`, which suggests that even with SIRCL the benefit is beginning to plateau around `T=16-32` for the current `1B` model scale.

So our answer is not that SIRCL enables unlimited scaling. Rather, it **substantially enlarges the stable operating range** of latent-token reasoning. For the current model and task scale, `T=16` already appears close to the best tradeoff, while `T=32` shows that the stable region extends further than it would without regularization.

This also explains why the gains in Table 1 at `T=6` are relatively modest. At short chain length, there is simply less drift to correct, so SIRCL's room for improvement is limited. Its value becomes much clearer when the latent-token budget increases and the unregularized baseline starts to fail.

We will incorporate these matched scaling results, together with the above success/failure boundary analysis, into the revised manuscript.
